Guided VLSM Practice Scenario

Understand how Variable Length Subnet Masking prevents IP address waste in enterprise networks.

Scenario Overview

Suppose an organization receives the network assignment 192.168.1.0/24 (Class C, 256 total IP addresses). The network administrator needs to carve out subnets for four distinct departments with varying host counts:

  • Department A: 50 hosts
  • Department B: 25 hosts
  • Department C: 10 hosts
  • Department D: 5 hosts

Step-by-Step Allocation Logic

1

Sort Requirements Largest to Smallest

Always allocate the largest subnet first so that boundary alignment works correctly. Order: Dept A (50), Dept B (25), Dept C (10), Dept D (5).

2

Allocate Department A (50 Hosts)

Needs ≥ 50 usable addresses. The smallest power of 2 accommodating 50 + 2 (network & broadcast) is 64 (26). Thus, prefix is 32 - 6 = /26.
Network: 192.168.1.0/26 | Range: 192.168.1.1 — 192.168.1.62 | Broadcast: 192.168.1.63

3

Allocate Department B (25 Hosts)

Starts immediately after Dept A ends. Next block starts at 192.168.1.64. Needs ≥ 25 usable IPs. 32 IPs (25) gives 30 usable hosts (/27).
Network: 192.168.1.64/27 | Range: 192.168.1.65 — 192.168.1.94 | Broadcast: 192.168.1.95

4

Allocate Department C (10 Hosts) & Department D (5 Hosts)

Dept C: Starts at 192.168.1.96. Needs 10 hosts → 16 IPs (24) → /28. Range: 192.168.1.97 — 192.168.1.110.
Dept D: Starts at 192.168.1.112. Needs 5 hosts → 8 IPs (23) → /29. Range: 192.168.1.113 — 192.168.1.118.
